# Product

Derived from `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-07-30-personal-editorial-deployment-portfolio-design.md`,
which remains the authoritative brief. This file is the short strategic summary
that design tooling reads first.

## Register

brand

Single-route personal portfolio. The design *is* the product: a visitor's
impression is the deliverable.

## Users

Hiring managers, deployment leaders, technical executives, recruiters, and peers
hiring for customer-facing AI delivery roles. They arrive skeptical, skim on a
laptop between meetings, and are deciding one thing: is this person worth an
interview?

The job to be done: verify that a Technical Project Manager with ten years of
delivery experience is a credible hire for AI deployment work — without being
asked to take a title claim on faith.

## Product Purpose

Make a qualified visitor want to interview Santiago.

Present him truthfully as a Technical Project Manager and Scrum Master with
financial and operational ownership plus growing hands-on depth in AI tooling,
and make the move toward AI deployment read as a continuation of his career
rather than a rebrand.

Success: the visitor can name what he owns, what he's proven, and what he's
preparing for, and can find the résumé and the email in one pass.

## Brand Personality

Direct, observant, self-aware.

First person. Short concrete sentences. The unglamorous parts of delivery named
out loud — budgets, staffing, dependencies, risk, hard conversations. Numbers
only when they are proof. Confidence comes from specificity, never from
adjectives. Sentence-case headings. Links are destinations or actions.

Emotional goal: the calm of someone explaining their actual work.

## Anti-references

- The generic "developer portfolio": terminal chrome, scramble text, count-up
  numbers, cycling verbs, ambient canvas, command palette, hover glow.
- Purple/blue "AI brand" palettes, gradients, glassmorphism.
- Consultant-speak: "bridging the gap", "at the intersection of", "seamless",
  "transformative", "unlock", "elevate".
- Framework theater: circular diagrams, five-step process graphics, dashboards.
  The pilot brief is a *document*, not a methodology chart.
- Desired-role language written as if it were current experience.
- Tomás Holtz's site (the original visual reference; do not recreate it).

## Design Principles

1. **Proof is attached to the claim.** Evidence sits next to the sentence it
   supports, never in a free-floating stats band.
2. **Show the artifact, not the framework.** When the copy says "five things
   written down", the page renders something that looks written down.
3. **Whitespace groups; rules are structural.** 1px hard rules are reserved for
   navigation, indexes, and real structural breaks — not decoration.
4. **The name is the identity device.** Oversized display typography carries
   recall; nothing else competes at that scale.
5. **Motion follows reading order.** One orchestrated hero moment and one
   repeated evidence behavior. No reveal-on-every-section reflex.
6. **Say what is current, what is exploratory, and what is next.** The interface
   itself must not overstate depth.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Single `h1` with a logical heading hierarchy, skip link preserved,
visible focus in the signal color with a high-contrast offset, full keyboard
reach in logical order, no color-only state, decorative rules and arrows hidden
from assistive tech, 44px touch targets, usable at 320px, and a static path for
every animation under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
